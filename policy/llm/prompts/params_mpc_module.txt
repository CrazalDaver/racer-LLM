MPC and vehicle
These describe how the MPC turns the reference points into commands and how
the simulated car responds. They do not say which values to choose.

MPC prediction model. The MPC predicts your car with a kinematic model in
track coordinates, state x = (s, e, heading error, v), commands
u = (throttle, steering), throttle in [0, 1] and steering in [-1, 1].
Predicted speed is kept within [0, 5] m/s.

Objective, minimised over the next N = 10 steps (1.0 s):

  J = sum_(k=0..N) q * ((s_k - s_ref,k)^2 + (e_k - e_ref,k)^2)
    + sum_(k=0..N-1) ||u_k - u_(k-1)||^2
    + 1000 * (track slack)^2 + 10000 * (separation slack)^2

where u_(-1) is the command applied in the previous 0.1 s.

Constraints:
- steering command change of at most 0.4 per 0.1 s step;
- a speed-dependent limit on steering, |v^2 * tan(steering)| <= 1.6 * 9.81 * 0.34;
- soft limit |e_k| <= 0.44 m (violations cost the track slack term);
- soft separation from the predicted positions of the nearest car ahead and
  the nearest car behind, (ds/0.428)^4 + (de/0.285)^4 >= 1 (violations cost
  the separation slack term).

Only the first command of the solution is applied; the problem is solved
again 0.1 s later from the new measured state.

Simulated car. The race itself uses a dynamic bicycle model with tyre forces
(states: position, heading, forward and sideways velocity, yaw rate), so the
real car can slide sideways and respond differently from the MPC's kinematic
prediction. Cars are 0.36 m long and 0.24 m wide. Recovery outside 0.55 m:
for an overshoot of o metres, each 0.1 s the speed is multiplied by
exp(-3 o) and the heading and steering are turned back toward the track.
