MPC and vehicle
These describe how the MPC turns the reference points into commands and how
the simulated car responds. They do not say which values to choose.

MPC prediction model. The MPC predicts your car with a kinematic bicycle model
in world coordinates, state (x, y, heading, v), commands u = (throttle a,
steering d):
  x' = v cos(heading), y' = v sin(heading),
  heading' = (v / 0.3) * tan(0.34 d),  v' = (20 / 4.65) a - 1.

Objective, minimised over the next N = 10 steps (1.0 s):

  J = sum_(k=0..9) q * ||(x_k, y_k) - p_(k+1)||^2 + sum_(k=0..9) (a_k^2 + d_k^2)

where p_1, ..., p_10 are the reference points and (x_0, y_0) is the current
position.

Constraints: |v_k^2 * tan(d_k)| <= 1.6 * 9.81 * 0.34, and predicted speed
within [0, 5] m/s. Throttle and steering have no bounds inside the MPC; the
car clips throttle to [0, 1] and steering to [-1, 1]. Only the first command
is applied; the problem is solved again 0.1 s later. After the MPC, if your
heading differs from the raceline direction a few points ahead by more than
30 degrees, steering is set fully toward the raceline.

Simulated car. The race itself uses a dynamic bicycle model with tyre forces
and one step of command delay, so the real car can slide sideways and respond
differently from the MPC's prediction. Cars are 0.36 m long and 0.24 m wide.
Contact: when two cars overlap, both speeds are multiplied by exp(-20 c) for
the car behind and exp(-5 c) for the car ahead, where c is the overlap area
measure. Recovery outside 0.55 m: for an overshoot of o metres, each 0.1 s the
speed is multiplied by exp(-3 o) and the heading and steering are turned
toward the track direction. Whenever the heading error exceeds 1 rad, the
throttle command gets +0.2.
