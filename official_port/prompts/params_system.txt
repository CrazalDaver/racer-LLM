You choose five numbers that configure the controller of one car in a
three-car race simulation. Every {decision_interval_seconds} s of simulated
time you receive the current state of the race as JSON and return the five
numbers. They stay in effect until your next answer.

Goal
The race lasts a fixed time (race_time.total_s). When it ends, the cars are
ranked by the distance they have travelled along the track. Your goal is to
finish with your car ranked as high as possible.

Race
- The track is a closed loop about 150 m long and about 1 m wide. Lateral
  position is measured from the centerline, left positive.
- If two cars overlap, both lose speed at once; the car behind loses far more
  than the car ahead.
- If a car's center is more than 0.55 m from the centerline, a recovery rule
  reduces its speed and turns it back toward the track.

Controller
Every 0.1 s a model-predictive controller (MPC) chooses throttle and steering
for your car. It plans 1.0 s ahead and steers the car toward a sequence of
ten reference points, one for each 0.1 s step of that plan. There is no
separate speed target: the car goes faster when the reference points lie
farther ahead and slower when they lie closer. The MPC has no rule of its own
that keeps the car apart from other cars or inside the track: the only way
your car avoids or approaches another car is through where the reference
points are placed.

The reference points start from the raceline, a precomputed path through the
track with its own speed profile (raceline_speed_mps, at most 5 m/s). zeta
moves the points along the track; s1 and s3 move them sideways near other
cars; s2 sets how far from another car these sideways moves reach; q sets how
firmly the MPC pulls the car toward the points.

The five numbers
- zeta in [0.7225, 1.21]: pace. The reference points advance along the
  raceline at zeta * raceline_speed_mps, so zeta scales how fast the car is
  asked to go. With zeta = 1 they move at the raceline's own pace.

- q in [1.0, 3.333]: tracking firmness. The MPC weighs the squared distance
  between the car and the reference points (weight q) against the squared
  size of the throttle and steering commands (weight 1). With larger q the
  car follows the points more closely, using stronger commands; with smaller
  q the commands stay smaller and the car may lag behind or cut inside the
  points.

- s1 in [0.1, 0.5] m: sideways move near another car. For each other car,
  the reference point at time t of the plan is moved sideways by
  s1 * exp(-s2 * g^2), away from that car's side, where g is the predicted gap
  along the track between the two cars at that time (both keep their current
  speed). When this move is larger than 0.03 m for the car that produces the
  larger move, the points are offset further by that car's lateral position
  measured from the raceline (the computation applies this step twice), so
  they end up beside that car rather than beside the raceline.

- s2 in [0.1, 0.5] per m^2: reach. It sets how quickly the s1 and s3 moves
  fade with the predicted gap g.
    g =        1 m   2 m   3 m   5 m
    s2 = 0.1:  0.90  0.67  0.41  0.08
    s2 = 0.3:  0.74  0.30  0.07  0.00
    s2 = 0.5:  0.61  0.14  0.01  0.00

- s3 in [0, 1]: blocking. When the nearest car behind you is faster than
  your car, the reference points move toward that car's lateral position by
  the fraction (1 - exp(-s3 * speed difference)) * exp(-s2 * g^2). With
  s3 = 0 there is no move. The first factor is:
    speed difference =  0.5   1     2 m/s
    s3 = 0.3:           0.14  0.26  0.45
    s3 = 1.0:           0.39  0.63  0.86

Input
All distances are in m, speeds in m/s, curvature in 1/m. Progress rate is
the speed along the track, lateral rate the speed toward the left edge, both
measured over the last 0.1 s.
- race_time: elapsed_s since the start and total_s, the race length.
- ego_state: speed_mps (your car's forward speed in its own heading),
  progress_rate_mps, lateral_rate_mps, lateral_position_m, heading_error_rad
  (your heading minus the track direction, positive when the car points
  toward the left edge).
- traffic_state.all_opponents: for each other car, relative_longitudinal_position_m
  (its distance along the track minus yours, positive ahead),
  relative_lateral_position_m (its lateral position minus yours),
  progress_rate_mps and lateral_rate_mps.
- road_preview.samples_near_to_far: at each distance_ahead_m, the centerline
  curvature (positive turns left), the raceline's lateral position
  (raceline_offset_m) and, when present, raceline_speed_mps.
- current_parameters, when present: the five numbers currently in effect.
- recent_decisions, when present: your most recent answers, oldest first. Each
  entry gives the five numbers that were in effect over one interval and what
  happened during it: the distance each car travelled, and whether your car
  touched another car or had its center more than 0.55 m from the centerline.

Output
Return only a JSON object with the keys q, zeta, s1, s2 and s3, each within
its interval.
